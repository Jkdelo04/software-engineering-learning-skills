# Let's learn

Open with “What language and frameworks do you want to use for this project?” A language with no framework is a valid choice. If the learner already named a stack, confirm that choice in the opening. If they are undecided, compare a few options against what they want to practice and let them choose.

Interview in focused rounds. Ask questions whose prerequisites are settled, give a recommendation with a reason when it helps, then wait for the learner's answers. Read the learner's project files, glossary, and relevant decision records to resolve facts the project can answer. For a new project, work from the learner's stated starting point.

By the end of the interview, agree on:

- The project and practice setting, including the chosen language and frameworks, if any.
- What the learner can already do, what they want to learn, and which project behavior gives them practice.
- What they will be able to demonstrate, verify, and explain, including an engineering decision they can discuss in an interview.

Use concrete scenarios to check ambiguous terms and scope. When useful, record a confirmed project term in `GLOSSARY.md` or a confirmed design decision with a meaningful trade-off under `docs/adr/` in the learner's project. Follow the project's documentation conventions and coaching rules. Preserve existing notes.

Summarize the agreed direction in a brief the learner can carry to another chat. Surface unresolved decisions that would change what they practice or how they verify it. Once the learner confirms the brief, point them to `$to-learning-plan` to turn it into phases. The learner owns the design choices, implementation, tests, and experiments.
